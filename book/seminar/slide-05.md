# The Crystal Eye Design

## Instrument geometry

The detector is built around a dome of **126 pixels** with a radius of about **15 cm**. The exploded view shows the parts of a pixel that work together when a photon arrives. When the photon deposits energy in a scintillator crystal, the crystal emits a flash of light. Silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) convert that light into an electrical signal the readout can measure. The separate **veto** helps flag events caused by charged particles.

The design figures shown in the seminar put the detector mass below **50 kg** and the total mass at **80 kg**. Those figures refer to different assemblies.

## Science-driven design

The dome is designed to cover more than **2π steradians** with a nearly uniform response. That coverage matters because a transient can arrive from any direction within the field of view. The proposed energy range is about **10 keV to 30 MeV**. The seminar quotes an effective area about **five times that of Fermi-GBM at 1 MeV** and a real-time localization target of about **one degree**. The compact, symmetric assembly also provides thermal and radiation protection for the SiPMs.

## Compatible mission profiles

The seminar considers several ways to carry the instrument. It could fly on a dedicated spacecraft in a near-equatorial low-Earth orbit at about **550 km**, operate aboard a space station, or serve as a gamma-ray burst monitor module on a larger satellite.
