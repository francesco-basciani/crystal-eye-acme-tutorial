The drawing labels two **3 cm** scintillator layers and a **0.5 cm** element. In the illustrated combination, the upper scintillator is GAGG and the lower one is LYSO. The layered design provides information about where energy was deposited as well as how much energy was recorded.

## Scintillator properties

| Property | LYSO | GAGG |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Density (g cm⁻³) | 7.25 | 6.6 |
| Refractive index | 1.82 | 1.91 |
| Light output (ph/MeV) | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| Wavelength of emission peak (nm) | 420 | 520 |
| Decay constant (ns) | 40 | 50 |
| Energy resolution (% at 662 keV) | 10.9 | 7 |

The candidate combinations are **GAGG + GAGG**, **GAGG + LYSO** and **LYSO + LYSO**. The table compares the properties that matter for the readout, including light output, peak emission wavelength and decay time. The source illustration flags the cost of the all-GAGG option and the high intrinsic noise of the all-LYSO option.

## Particle trajectories

Read the particle sketches as five ways a track may cross or stop in the detector. Cases **a**, **b** and **c** show a downward hard X-ray, a lower-energy gamma ray and a medium-energy gamma ray. Cases **d** and **e** show a downward lower-energy charged particle and a high-energy charged particle. Their different paths help explain why the detector records the pattern across layers.
