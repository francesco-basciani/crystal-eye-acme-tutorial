# In-orbit Background

## Orbit and incident background

The background example on this page assumes a low-Earth orbit at **550 km** altitude and **20°** inclination. These orbital conditions define the environment used for the incident radiation spectra.

~~~{figure} ../figures/seminar/figure-13-1.webp
:alt: Major radiation background fluxes in low-Earth orbit
:figclass: ce-seminar-figure

Major radiation background fluxes in low-Earth orbit.
~~~

The incident-flux figure compares the main background components in low-Earth orbit. The seminar credits the source spectra to Cumani et al. (2019).

[Background for a gamma-ray satellite in a low-Earth orbit](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06944)

## Detector maps by background component

~~~{figure} ../figures/seminar/figure-13-3.webp
:alt: Component detector maps, total background and the shared counts-per-second color scale
:figclass: ce-seminar-figure

Component detector maps, total background and the shared counts-per-second color scale.
~~~

The maps show where events are recorded across the detector for cosmic photons, albedo photons, albedo neutrons, secondary protons, secondary electrons and secondary positrons. The total map combines those contributions. Use the common color scale to compare count rates between maps.
